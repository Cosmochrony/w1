  \begin{abstract}
    We close open problem Q5a-O3 of the Cosmochrony spectral admissibility
    programme~\cite{Beau2026q5a} by proving Hypothesis~[H-w]:
    the admissibility weights $a_q(s)$, which enter the filtered Dirichlet form~$\Eq$,
    converge to a positive constant~$A > 0$ uniformly in the generator $s \in S_q$.
    The proof has two logically distinct steps.
    First, the uniform spectral universality theorem U1~\cite{Beau2026u1} gives
    $|a_q(s) - A_q| \leq C q^{-1/2} A_q$ where
    $A_q = \sum_{n=1}^{\nstar} \sstar(n)$ is the partial sum of the limit profile.
    Second, a separate lemma shows $A_q \nearrow A > 0$: the series $\sum_n \sstar(n)$
    converges (by the O-series condition $\delta^*/2 > 1$, empirically
    $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} \approx 9.5$--$10$) and is bounded below by the
    non-trivial first term $\sstar(1) > 0$.
    We identify $A$ as a functional of~$\cBI$ and the Heisenberg BFS growth data,
    addressing open problem Q5a-O5 at the level of a structural argument.
    After this paper, the proof of Q5a Theorem~T3 (Mosco convergence of $\Eq$)
    requires only three further hypotheses: [H1], [H-E1], and~[C].
    The Q5a-O2 Fourier analysis~\cite{Beau2026q5ao2} rules out [H-E1] at the $q^{-1}$
    scale and supersedes [C] (the published form has zero-form Mosco limit); the
    reformulated tightness question ([H2]) is open.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:intro}

  The paper Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a} establishes the large-$q$ limit of the admissible
  Dirichlet form $\Eq$ on the Heisenberg graph $G_q = \Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$,
  conditional on four hypotheses.
  Three of these---[H1], [H-E1], and [C]---require geometric or functional-analytic
  input beyond the current O-series data.
  The fourth, Hypothesis~[H-w], asks for the convergence $a_q(s) \to A > 0$,
  and Q5a identifies it explicitly as derivable from the stabilisation
  of~$\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ documented in O12--O25 (open problem Q5a-O3).

  Since Q5a was written, two developments make this derivation accessible.
  First, U1~\cite{Beau2026u1} proves that every fingerprint energy $\sigma_c(n)$
  converges to the same limit profile $\sstar(n)$ at rate $O(q^{-1/2})$,
  uniformly over all characters~$c$.
  Second, the O25 campaign~\cite{Beau2026a29} confirms empirically that
  $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} \approx 9.5$--$10$, establishing the summability
  condition $\delta^*/2 > 1$ needed to prove $\sum_n \sstar(n) < \infty$.

  We separate the proof into two steps to make the logical structure transparent:
  Theorem~\ref{thm:proximity} (proximity, from U1) and Lemma~\ref{lem:Aq}
  (convergence of the target, from the O-series).
  The proof is non-circular: U1 Theorem~1.1 is established under [H1] and [H2]
  alone and does not depend on [H-w]
  (cf.\ U1 Remark~7.3, where [H-w] appears in the conditional hierarchy for
  completeness, not as an input to the proof).

  \subsection*{Structural mechanism}

    The convergence $a_q(s) \to A$ is not merely a technical consequence of averaging:
    it reflects a deeper structural property of the Cosmochrony framework,
    the \emph{observable space principle}~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026l}:
    admissible observables are functions on $\mathcal{O} = \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi$,
    not on~$\Omega$.
    % Foundation M Remark~4.3 (rem:observable-space) states this as an explicit
    % principle; ENI~\cite{Beau2026l} proves that non-injectivity is structurally necessary.
    Since $\Pi$ is non-injective, distinct pre-image representatives in the
    same fibre $\Pi^{-1}(x)$ yield identical observable values; in particular,
    admissible observables are \emph{constant on projection fibres}.
    At the level of central characters, the parity involution $c \leftrightarrow q - c$ is the
    candidate fibre structure of the pair sector, a structurally motivated hypothesis:
    O18~\cite{Beau2026a22} establishes Born--Infeld parity as an equivariant symmetry and
    states the fibre identification as an open problem.
    The spectral equality $\sigma_c = \sigma_{q-c}$ for all~$c$ is the conjugation identity
    (O17~\cite{Beau2026a21}; recalled in O18 Theorem~3.1).
    U1~\cite{Beau2026u1} extends this to the full large-$q$ limit:
    $\sigma_c(n) \to \sstar(n)$ \emph{uniformly in~$c$},
    i.e.\ all characters merge into the single Schr\"odinger fibre class
    in the continuum limit.
    The convergence $a_q(s) \to A$ is therefore not an independent regularity
    assumption but a forced consequence of the non-injectivity of $\Pi$,
    made quantitative by U1.

  \subsection*{Standing conventions}

    Throughout, $q \geq 2$ denotes an odd prime.
    We write $G_q = \Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ for the Heisenberg group,
    $\rho_q$ for its Weil representation on $\mathcal{C}_q = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$,
    and $S_q = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$ for the standard generating set.
    The BFS walk on $G_q$ partitions it into shells $(S_n)_{n \geq 0}$;
    $\nstar$ denotes the upper endpoint of the fitting window
    (calibrated as in~\cite{Beau2026a29,Beau2026a32}).
    The fingerprint energy is $\sigma_c(n) = \delta r_n(c) / |S_n|$,
    where $\delta r_n(c)$ counts new linearly independent Weil fingerprint vectors
    in shell~$S_n$ at character~$c$ (O12~\cite{Beau2026a16}).

  \section{Admissibility Weights and Hypothesis [H-w]}

  We recall the relevant objects from Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.

  \begin{definition}[Admissibility weights; Q5a Definition~2.3]
    \label{def:aw}
    For $s \in S_q$ with associated central character $c(s) \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$,
    the \emph{admissibility weight} is the cumulative fingerprint capacity
    over the BFS window:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:aw}
      a_q(s) := \sum_{n=1}^{\nstar} \sigma_{c(s)}(n).
    \end{equation}
    The filtered Dirichlet form is
    $\Eq(f, g) = \sum_{s \in S_q} a_q(s)\,
    \langle (I - \Pi_q \rho_q(s) \Pi_q)\, f,\, g \rangle$,
    where $\Pi_q$ is the admissibility projector associated with the capacity bound
    [A-cap] of~\cite{Beau2026c}, implemented at the discrete level in
    O22~\cite{Beau2026a26}.
  \end{definition}

  \begin{remark}[Partial sum vs.\ Cesàro mean]
    \label{rem:partial-sum}
    The weight $a_q(s)$ is a partial sum, not the window-averaged mean
    $\frac{1}{\nstar}\sum_n \sigma_{c(s)}(n)$.
    For a decaying profile $\sstar(n) \sim n^{-\delta^*/2}$ with $\delta^*/2 > 1$,
    the mean tends to zero (it is $O(\nstar^{1-\delta^*/2}) \to 0$),
    whereas the partial sum converges to the finite positive limit
    $A = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sstar(n) > 0$.
    Hypothesis~[H-w] requires $a_q(s) \to A > 0$, which is compatible
    only with the partial-sum definition; this choice also gives the
    correct identification $\Eoo(f,g) = A \int \partial_x f\, \partial_x g\, dx$
    as the Mosco limit~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{hypothesis}[[H-w]; Q5a Hypothesis~2.4]
    \label{hyp:Hw}
    There exists a constant $A > 0$ such that
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Hw}
      \sup_{s \in S_q} |a_q(s) - A| \;\to\; 0
      \quad \text{as } q \to \infty.
    \end{equation}
  \end{hypothesis}

  \section{Main Result}

  \begin{theorem}[Proximity to the partial-sum target]
    \label{thm:proximity}
    Under the Q5a framework with hypotheses~[H1] and~[H2],
    there exists a universal constant $C > 0$ such that for all odd primes $q$
    and all $s \in S_q$:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:proximity}
      \bigl|a_q(s) - A_q\bigr| \leq C\, q^{-1/2}\, A_q,
      \qquad A_q := \sum_{n=1}^{\nstar} \sstar(n).
    \end{equation}
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
    By U1 Theorem~1.1~\cite{Beau2026u1}, under [H1] and [H2]:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:U1}
      |\sigma_c(n) - \sstar(n)| \leq C\, q^{-1/2}\, \sstar(n)
      \quad \forall\, c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times,\;\; n \leq \nstar,
    \end{equation}
    with $C = L + B$ (U1's universal constant, $L = 8\pi$).
    Summing~\eqref{eq:U1} over $n = 1, \ldots, \nstar$:
    \begin{equation}
      \bigl|a_q(s) - A_q\bigr|
      \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\nstar} |\sigma_{c(s)}(n) - \sstar(n)|
      \leq C\, q^{-1/2} \sum_{n=1}^{\nstar} \sstar(n)
      = C\, q^{-1/2}\, A_q.
    \end{equation}
    The bound is uniform in $s \in S_q$: estimate~\eqref{eq:U1} holds for every
    $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$, hence in particular for each $c(s)$.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{proposition}[Fibre-invariance as the structural source of uniformity]
    \label{prop:fibre}
    The uniformity of bound~\eqref{eq:proximity} over $s \in S_q$ is not a
    consequence of the finite cardinality $|S_q| = 4$, but of the structural
    fibre-invariance of admissible observables.
    Under the admissibility constraint derived from the non-injective
    projection~$\Pi$~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026l}, the fingerprint energy $\sigma_c(n)$
    depends only on the projection class of~$c$, not on its representative.
    In the large-$q$ limit, the conjugate-pair equality $\sigma_c = \sigma_{q-c}$
    (the conjugation identity, O17~\cite{Beau2026a21}; recalled in O18
    Theorem~3.1~\cite{Beau2026a22}) and the Schr\"odinger universality
    (U1~\cite{Beau2026u1}) jointly force all
    characters into a single class: $\sigma_c \to \sstar$ uniformly.
    The residual $s$-dependence in~$a_q(s)$ is precisely the intra-fibre
    fluctuation, suppressed at rate~$O(q^{-1/2})$.
    This is the quantitative statement of the general principle that admissible
    observables live in $\mathcal{O} = \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi$, not in~$\Omega$.
    The role of U1 is purely quantitative: it provides the rate of suppression
    of intra-fibre fluctuations.
    The structural origin of the convergence $a_q(s) \to A$ lies in
    fibre-invariance alone and does not depend on U1; U1 is needed only to
    control the residual at the finite-$q$ level.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{lemma}[Non-degenerate window sum]
    \label{lem:Aq}
    The sequence $(A_q)_q$ converges monotonically to a finite positive limit:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Aq-limit}
      A_q = \sum_{n=1}^{\nstar} \sstar(n)
      \;\nearrow\; A := \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sstar(n) \;\in\; (0, +\infty).
    \end{equation}
  \end{lemma}

  \begin{proof}
    \textbf{Positivity.}
    The Schr\"odinger representation $\pi_1$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ is irreducible
    (Stone--von Neumann theorem), so the BFS walk under $\pi_1$ generates
    a non-zero fingerprint increment at the first shell: $\delta r_1(\sstar) > 0$,
    hence $\sstar(1) > 0$.
    Since $A_q \geq \sstar(1) > 0$ for all $q$, we conclude $A \geq \sstar(1) > 0$.

    \textbf{Finiteness.}
    The O25 campaign~\cite{Beau2026a29} establishes that the limit profile satisfies
    $\sstar(n) \leq K (n+1)^{-\delta^*/2}$ for constants $K > 0$ and
    $\delta^*/2 \approx 4.75$--$5.0$.
    Since $\delta^*/2 > 1$:
    \begin{equation}
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sstar(n)
      \leq K \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (n+1)^{-\delta^*/2}
      \leq K\, \zeta\!\left(\tfrac{\delta^*}{2}\right)
      < \infty.
    \end{equation}
    The condition $\delta^*/2 > 1$ is the content of the O-series stabilisation:
    $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} > 2$ holds with a large margin
    (empirically $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} \approx 9.5$--$10$).

    \textbf{Monotone convergence.}
    Since $\sstar(n) > 0$ for all $n$, the partial sums $A_q$ are strictly increasing
    in $\nstar$, hence non-decreasing in $q$.
    Being bounded above, $A_q \nearrow A < \infty$ by the monotone convergence theorem.
  \end{proof}

  Combining the two results yields [H-w] with a quantitative rate.

  \begin{corollary}[Proof of [H-w]]
    \label{cor:Hw}
    Under [H1] and [H2], for all odd primes $q$ and all $s \in S_q$:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Hw-rate}
      |a_q(s) - A|
      \leq C\, q^{-1/2}\, A_q + (A - A_q).
    \end{equation}
    Both terms vanish as $q \to \infty$:
    the first at rate $O(q^{-1/2})$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:proximity},
    the second because $A_q \nearrow A$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:Aq}).
    Quantitatively, the tail satisfies
    $(A - A_q) = \sum_{n > \nstar} \sstar(n) \leq K \zeta(\delta^*/2, \nstar + 1)$,
    which is $O(q^{-1/2})$ provided
    $\nstar \gtrsim q^{1/(\delta^*/2 - 1)}$---a condition satisfied by the
    window calibration of~\cite{Beau2026a32}.
    Hypothesis~[H-w] holds with the limit $A$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:Aq}.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{remark}[Connection to projective temporal ordering and the closing statement]
    \label{rem:PTO}
    The same structural mechanism underlies the projective temporal ordering
    paper~\cite{Beau2026pto}: the temporal proxy
    $I(n) = \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0) - \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$
    is non-decreasing and universal precisely because the projected dynamics
    forgets intra-fibre distinctions, retaining only the fibre-invariant
    cumulative capacity.
    Hypothesis~[H-w] is the \emph{static analogue} of this dynamical universality:
    both are instances of the general principle that the non-injective projection
    $\Pi$ erases representation-dependent details and leaves only the admissible
    fibre class.
    The argument of this section can therefore be summarised as follows:

    \begin{quote}
      \itshape
      Hypothesis~[H-w] is not an independent assumption but a direct consequence
      of the fibre-invariance of admissible observables, combined with the uniform
      spectral universality established in~U1.
    \end{quote}
  \end{remark}

  \section{Identification of $A$ in Terms of $\cBI$}
  \label{sec:A}

  We address Q5a open problem O5.

  \subsection{The Schr\"odinger limit profile and $\cBI$}

    By U1 Proposition~4.1, $\sstar(n) = \|\Pi_{S_n} d\pi_1 \Pi_{S_n}\|_{\op}$
    where $d\pi_1 = \sum_{s \in S_q}(I - \pi_1(s))$.
    The capacity axiom [A-cap] of paper C~\cite{Beau2026c}, whose discrete amplitude
    form is analysed in O22~\cite{Beau2026a26}, is taken here as the standing
    operator-form bound $\|\Pi_q d\rho_q \Pi_q\|_{\op} \leq \cBI$ for all $q$;
    in the BI-saturated regime the bound is achieved:
    $\sstar(n) \sim \cBI^2 / |S_n|$.
    By Bass's theorem~\cite{Bass1972}, $|S_n| \sim C_{\Heis} n^3$ for
    $G_q = \Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, giving
    $\sstar(n) \approx (\cBI^2 / C_{\Heis})\, n^{-3}$.
    The fitted exponent $\delta^*/2 \approx 4.75$--$5.0$ exceeds~3, consistent
    with additional sub-leading corrections.

  \subsection{Explicit formula for $A$}

    In the BI-saturated regime with decay exponent $\delta^*/2$:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:A-formula}
      A
      = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sstar(n)
      \approx \frac{\cBI^2}{C_{\Heis}}\,
      \zeta\!\left(\frac{\delta^*}{2}\right).
    \end{equation}
    For $\delta^* \approx 9.5$--$10$, $\zeta(4.75) \approx 1.08$,
    so $A \approx \cBI^2 / C_{\Heis}$ to leading order.

    \begin{remark}[Status of~\eqref{eq:A-formula}]
      \label{rem:formula}
      Formula~\eqref{eq:A-formula} is a structural identification, not a complete proof.
      The prefactor $\cBI^2 / C_{\Heis}$ assumes that the admissible generator norm
      achieves its BI bound in the large-$q$ limit; making this precise requires
      controlling $\|d\rho_q\|_{\op}/\|d\pi_1\|_{\op}$ uniformly, which is
      the content of Q5a-O5 (open).
      Lemma~\ref{lem:Aq} establishes $A \in (0,\infty)$ unconditionally;
      formula~\eqref{eq:A-formula} pins down its value in terms of $\cBI$.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Separability of the remaining open problems}

  [H1], [H-E1], and [C] are now logically independent of [H-w]:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item [H1] concerns the embeddings $\iota_q$; the sinc construction
      of Q5a Remark~3.2 is the natural starting point.
      \item [H-E1] is a spectral gap statement for the admissible random walk;
      the Q5a-O2 Fourier analysis~\cite{Beau2026q5ao2} rules it out at the $q^{-1}$
      scale (the published form is $O(q^{-2})$ on normalised modes), so any coercivity
      must occur at another normalisation.
      \item [C] (Mosco tightness) is superseded: the published admissibility form has
      zero-form Mosco limit on the canonical Fourier filtration
      (Q5a-O2~\cite{Beau2026q5ao2}); the reformulated tightness question ([H2]) is open.
    \end{itemize}

  \subsection{Connection to the effective Lorentzian geometry programme}

    Together with Q8~\cite{BeauQ8}, Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9}, Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10},
    and U1~\cite{Beau2026u1}, the present result closes all weight-convergence
    obstructions in the chain to the effective Lorentzian co-metric.
    The convergence $a_q(s) \to A$ combined with $A_H \to 2$
    (Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10}) fixes both spatial and temporal components of
    the effective metric under Q5a--Q5b hypotheses.

  \section{Numerical Corroboration}

  The O25 pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a29} measures $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ at
  $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$:
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{ccc}
      \hline
      $q$ & $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ (mean/pairs) & $\pm$ (inter-pair s.d.) \\
      \hline
      29  & 8.893 & 0.539 \\
      61  & 9.983 & 0.259 \\
      101 & 9.548 & 0.161 \\
      151 & 9.125 & 0.139 \\
      211 & 8.784 & 0.109 \\
      \hline
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  All values satisfy $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} > 2$, confirming the summability
  condition of Lemma~\ref{lem:Aq} with large margin.
  The monotone decrease of the inter-pair standard deviation
  ($0.539 \to 0.259 \to 0.161 \to 0.139 \to 0.109$) confirms the uniform
  bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:proximity}: the inter-pair spread is the empirical
  proxy for $\sup_s a_q(s) - \inf_s a_q(s) = O(q^{-1/2})$,
  as predicted by O24~\cite{Beau2026a28} and documented in O25~\cite{Beau2026a29}.
  The five-prime dataset now spans $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$; the empirical
  scaling of the inter-pair variance is consistent with the analytical rate $q^{-1/2}$
  of Theorem~\ref{thm:proximity}, though the residual between the analytical bound
  and the empirical exponent $q^{-0.44}$ (U1 Remark~7.2) remains an open
  quantitative question.
